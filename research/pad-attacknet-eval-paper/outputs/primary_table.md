Notes.

1. Patience 4, fixed threshold 0.5 (effective threshold 0.5 for every model in both BN states, checked in
   `units.csv`). BN states: ordinary, and re-estimated on the fitting set only. The means of (a) and every
   mean of (b) and (c) are computed from the exact fractions of `units.csv`; the means of (b) and (c) are
   also those of `results.json` (the script stops if they differ).
2. Subject intervals: percentiles 2.5–97.5 of 1,000 resamples of the 16 test subjects pooled over the 4 folds, the
   same resamples for every network and both BN states; valid resamples 926 for ACER, 950 for APCER and 940 for
   BPCER, in every block (checked). They are individual 95% intervals: no correction for multiplicity, no
   simultaneous coverage over the contrasts, variations or comparisons. They are copied from `results.json`: they
   cannot be recomputed from the public files, which hold no per-subject counts. No interval is published for the
   rates of (a).
3. Reading of each contrast (`PROTOCOL.md` §6 (c), from the interval of its ACER over the three seeds
   together): "... lower than zero" if the interval lies entirely below zero, "... higher than zero" if
   entirely above, "unresolved" if it contains zero (a bound at zero contains it). One conclusion per
   AttackNet, no joint statement on the three (quoted from `results.json`):
   - AttackNetV1: "On these checkpoints and test frames, the ACER variation with this BN re-estimation (re-estimated
     minus ordinary BN) is algebraically higher for AttackNetV1 than for LivenessNet; it does not identify a cause."
   - AttackNetV2_1: "On these checkpoints and test frames, the ACER variation with this BN re-estimation
     (re-estimated minus ordinary BN) is algebraically higher for AttackNetV2_1 than for LivenessNet; it does not
     identify a cause."
   - AttackNetV2_2: "On these checkpoints and test frames, the ACER variation with this BN re-estimation
     (re-estimated minus ordinary BN) is algebraically higher for AttackNetV2_2 than for LivenessNet; it does not
     identify a cause."
   The five variations are reported with their intervals and no reading.
4. Test frames per fold (`denominators` of `results.json`, checked against `units.csv`): fold 1: 3,242 frames (1,819
   attack, 1,423 bona fide), 4 subjects; fold 2: 3,114 frames (1,744 attack, 1,370 bona fide), 4 subjects; fold 3:
   3,439 frames (2,052 attack, 1,387 bona fide), 4 subjects; fold 4: 2,819 frames (1,894 attack, 925 bona fide), 4
   subjects. Each fold is scored by its three seeds, so the attack and bona fide frames of a network are summed over
   its units (the APCER and BPCER denominators); the 16 subjects are pooled over the folds. Summed over the 12 units:
   37,842 test frames, 22,527 attack and 15,315 bona fide; each test frame counts once per seed (12,614 distinct test
   frames: 7,509 attack and 5,105 bona fide).
5. 2 models decide every test frame as the same class (LivenessNet fold 1 seed 42, patience 4, ordinary BN, threshold
   0.5: every frame attack; LivenessNet fold 4 seed 44, patience 4, ordinary BN, threshold 0.5: every frame attack;
   ACER 1/2); they enter the means like the others; both are inside the ordinary-BN mean of LivenessNet, and so
